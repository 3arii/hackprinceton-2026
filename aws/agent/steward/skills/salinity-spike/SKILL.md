# Salinity spike

## Hypotheses to test
1. **Road salt**: snow or ice in the past week, then melt or rain; chloride-rich runoff from Route 1 and campus roads; the river's conductance rises too. Common December–March, rare otherwise.
2. **Wastewater**: sewage treatment plants raise conductivity steadily; check Stony Brook RSA and Stage II permits for violations.
3. **Industrial discharge**: abrupt, localized, near a permitted facility.

## Evidence
- Snowfall in the weather history, and the season.
- River specific conductance (µS/cm) converted to PSU; a freshwater reading of 0.15–0.30 PSU is normal here.
- Which buoys are high, and which facility lies upstream or nearest.
- Facility compliance history: quarters in noncompliance, significant-noncomplier status.

## Thresholds
- Above 0.5 PSU is unusual for this lake; chloride above 230 mg/L (about 0.4 PSU) is the EPA chronic aquatic-life criterion.

## Community signals
Community (Bluesky) confirms snow, ice and road salting; posts about a spill or a broken main point elsewhere.
