# Turbidity event

## Hypotheses to test
1. **Storm runoff**: heavy rain in the last 1–3 days (over 20 mm in a day is a lot here), river turbidity and discharge rising, highest at the buoy nearest the stream mouth (buoy 1, west), all buoys elevated.
2. **Wind or boat resuspension**: strong wind (over 40 km/h), short-lived, no rain, river normal.
3. **Construction or earthworks runoff**: localized, persists in dry weather, near a disturbed shoreline.
4. **Spill or discharge**: localized, no rain, often with other metrics moving (salinity, microplastics).
5. **Algal bloom**: warm, calm, sunny weeks; satellite chlorophyll index (NDCI) up; turbidity rises slowly.

## Evidence that separates them
- Rain totals and river discharge: runoff almost always shows in both.
- Spatial pattern: runoff grades away from inflows; a spill centres on its source.
- Satellite NDTI (turbidity index) on the newest clear scene against the median of recent scenes; NDCI for algae.
- The river gauge lags the lake by about a day because it is 15 km downstream.

## Thresholds
- Over 50 NTU is visibly muddy; over 100 NTU harms fish gills and buries habitat if it lasts.
- Natural runoff peaks fade within 2–4 days. If turbidity keeps climbing in dry weather, warn.

## Community signals
Community (Bluesky) shows storms, flooding and visible muddy water; posts about construction, a spill or dumping near the shore point to a human source.
